# Greetland Community Group — Northern England National Parks Volunteer Outing

Welcome to the project hub for the **Greetland Community Group's National Park Volunteer Outing**! 

Based in Greetland (Calderdale, West Yorkshire), our community group is organizing a joint conservation and volunteer day in one of the spectacular national parks located in Northern England or the surrounding South Pennines region. This repository coordinates event research, volunteer registration, travel logistics, and equipment needs.

## Overview & Regional Context

* **Base Location:** Greetland / West Vale, Calderdale, West Yorkshire (HX4)
* **Accessibility:** Direct access to the M62 corridor (Junction 22/24), A629 towards Huddersfield/Peak District or Halifax/Skipton, and local rail connections via Halifax, Brighouse, and Sowerby Bridge.
* **Target Audience:** Greetland residents, families, and community members of diverse fitness levels looking to give back to Northern England's protected landscapes.

## National Parks & Community Events in Northern England

Northern England boasts four official National Parks alongside designated National Landscapes (formerly AONBs) and National Trust estate lands. Below is a detailed assessment of each park, drive times from Greetland, and types of community volunteer events available:

### 1. Peak District National Park
* **Distance from Greetland:** ~25–35 miles (approx. 45–60 minutes drive south via A629 / A6024 Holme Moss or M62/A628 Woodhead Pass).
* **Key Focus Areas:** Dark Peak (Kinder Scout, Bleaklow, Black Hill), Upper Derwent Valley, Hope Valley (Edale, Castleton).
* **Upcoming Community & Volunteer Event Types:**
  - **Moors for the Future Partnership (Community Science & Peat Restoration):** Planting sphagnum moss plugs, installing coir logs to re-wet moorland peat, and monitoring biodiversity (cotton grass, bilberry, mountain hares).
  - **Peak District Conservation Volunteers (PDCV) & Ranger Days:** Rebuilding traditional gritstone dry stone walls, replacing degraded waymarkers, and repairing footpaths on high-traffic sections of the Pennine Way.
  - **Invasive Species Eradication:** Himalayan balsam pulling along the River Derwent and tributary valleys during late spring/early summer.
  - **"Miles without Stiles" Accessibility Projects:** Clearing encroaching vegetation and leveling paths to ensure countryside access for people of all mobilities.
* **Why It’s Ideal for Greetland:** It is the closest National Park to Greetland, minimizing transit time and allowing a full day of productive volunteering.

### 2. Yorkshire Dales National Park
* **Distance from Greetland:** ~35–50 miles (approx. 55–75 minutes drive north via A629 through Halifax, Keighley, and Skipton onto the A65/B6160).
* **Key Focus Areas:** Southern Dales (Wharfedale, Bolton Abbey, Grassington, Malham Cove, Burnsall).
* **Upcoming Community & Volunteer Event Types:**
  - **Dales Volunteers Practical Days:** Dry stone wall restoration and traditional limestone walling in pastoral landscapes.
  - **Woodland & Meadow Conservation (Friends of the Dales):** Ancient woodland restoration at Grass Wood (scrub clearing, hazel coppicing) and wildflower hay meadow restoration (yellow rattle seed scattering and brush harvesting).
  - **Footpath Maintenance & Erosion Control:** Path re-surfacing and drain clearing along iconic routes such as Malham Landscape Trail and Three Peaks paths.
  - **River Wharfe Cleanups:** Community riverbank litter sweeps and water health surveys.
* **Why It’s Ideal for Greetland:** Skipton is easily reachable; southern Dales sites provide accessible terrain suitable for mixed-ability groups, accompanied by welcoming village hubs.

### 3. North York Moors National Park
* **Distance from Greetland:** ~70–85 miles (approx. 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours drive via M62 East and A19/A64).
* **Key Focus Areas:** Sutton Bank, Danby Lodge National Park Centre, Goathland, Dalby Forest.
* **Upcoming Community & Volunteer Event Types:**
  - **Heather & Peat Management:** Bracken control, burning/cutting management to maintain heather moorland for nesting curlew and red grouse.
  - **Coast-to-Coast / Cleveland Way Trail Upkeep:** Boardwalk repairs across wet moor sections and path brush-clearing.
  - **Dark Skies Friendly Lighting & Community Tree Planting:** Planting native broadleaf trees to counter ash dieback and participating in community stargazing conservation events.
* **Consideration:** Best suited as an extended full-day expedition due to the ~4-hour round-trip travel time.

### 4. Lake District National Park
* **Distance from Greetland:** ~85–100 miles (approx. 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours drive via M62 West and M6 North).
* **Key Focus Areas:** South Lakes (Kendal, Windermere, Ullswater, Staveley).
* **Upcoming Community & Volunteer Event Types:**
  - **Fix the Fells Volunteer Workdays:** Pitching stone paths, clearing cross-drains, and repairing erosion scars caused by heavy visitor footfall.
  - **Temperate Rainforest Restoration:** Planting sessile oak, birch, and hazel saplings with the Cumbria Wildlife Trust and National Trust.
  - **Lakeshore Cleanups:** Community kayak and walking litter pick-ups around Windermere and Coniston Water.
* **Consideration:** Incredible scenery and rewarding high-impact work, but requires an early departure from Greetland (07:00).

### 5. Surrounding Region: South Pennines & Marsden Moor (Immediate Neighbor)
* **Distance from Greetland:** ~10–14 miles (approx. 20–25 minutes drive via B6112 / A640 / A62).
* **Key Focus Areas:** National Trust Marsden Moor Estate, Standedge, Scammonden Reservoir.
* **Upcoming Community & Volunteer Event Types:**
  - **Post-Wildfire Moorland Recovery:** Sphagnum moss planting and peat gully blocking directly on our local Pennine doorstep.
  - **Community Fire Watch Patrols & Invasive Rhododendron Clearance.**
* **Why It’s Ideal for Greetland:** Serves as a fantastic backup or half-day outing option requiring negligible travel overhead.

## Comprehensive Logistics Checklist

### Pre-Event Planning
- [ ] Select final date and park option based on community group poll.
- [ ] Contact park volunteer coordinator (Peak District NP Ranger Service or Yorkshire Dales NP Volunteer Office) to register the group and confirm task location.
- [ ] Confirm task risk assessment and ensure community group public liability insurance coverage.
- [ ] Monitor weather forecasts (Met Office / Mountain Weather Information Service - MWIS) 72h and 24h prior.
- [ ] Appoint Day Lead, First Aider, and Logistics Coordinator.

### Transport & Route Logistics
- [ ] **Meeting Point:** Greetland Community Centre car park (Rochdale Road, Greetland, HX4 8JG) or West Vale public car park.
- [ ] **Departure Time:** 08:00 AM (sharp) to arrive on-site for 09:15–09:30 AM briefings.
- [ ] **Vehicle Allocations:**
  - Finalize carpooling passenger pairings (aim for 3–4 persons per car to minimize emissions).
  - Check park parking permits, pay-and-display fees, or ranger base parking authorization.
  - If group size exceeds 16, obtain quotes for 17-seat community minibus hire (e.g., Calderdale Community Transport).
- [ ] Distribute offline maps and designated driver contact sheet.

### Supplies, PPE & Field Kit

#### What Individual Volunteers Must Bring (Personal Kit)
- [ ] Sturdy walking boots or safety wellingtons with deep-tread soles (broken-in, waterproof).
- [ ] Waterproof jacket and waterproof overtrousers (essential for Pennine/Dales weather).
- [ ] Warm layers (fleece, thermal base layers, wool socks; avoid pure cotton/denim).
- [ ] Warm beanie hat and waterproof work gloves (heavy-duty thornproof/leather work gloves).
- [ ] Sun protection (sunscreen, sunglasses, cap — upland UV can be strong even when overcast).
- [ ] Daypack (20–30L) lined with a dry-bag or waterproof cover.
- [ ] Packed lunch, high-energy trail snacks (flapjacks, nuts, fruit).
- [ ] Reusable water bottle (minimum 1.5–2 litres) and hot drink thermos flask.
- [ ] Personal medication (inhalers, antihistamines, EpiPens).

#### Group Supplies & Safety Kit (Organiser Provided)
- [ ] Comprehensive outdoor Group First Aid Kit (including blister packs, burn gel, eye wash).
- [ ] Tick removal tools and antiseptic wipes.
- [ ] Hi-visibility vests (with Greetland Community Group identification).
- [ ] Hand sanitizer, wet wipes, and bio-degradable waste sacks (Leave No Trace).
- [ ] Heavy-duty tool set (spades, loppers, bow saws, mattocks — coordinated with park rangers).
- [ ] Emergency bivvy bag / group storm shelter (Bothy bag) for upland contingencies.
- [ ] Laminated task sheets, ranger emergency phone numbers, and grid references.

### On-the-Day Schedule Example
* **07:45 AM:** Meet at Greetland Community Centre; check-in and vehicle loading.
* **08:00 AM:** Depart Greetland.
* **09:15 AM:** Arrive at Park Ranger Station / Field Base; comfort break.
* **09:30 AM:** Ranger welcome, health & safety toolbox talk, tool distribution.
* **10:00 AM – 12:30 PM:** Morning volunteer session (e.g., stone walling, sphagnum planting, trail works).
* **12:30 PM – 01:15 PM:** Group picnic lunch and social time.
* **01:15 PM – 03:30 PM:** Afternoon volunteer session and task completion.
* **03:30 PM – 04:00 PM:** Tool cleaning, count-back, ranger debrief, and photo session.
* **04:15 PM:** Optional stop at a local village tearoom/pub for tea and cake.
* **05:45 PM:** Return arrival at Greetland Community Centre.

### Post-Event Wrap-up
- [ ] Clean and inspect all communal tools.
- [ ] Record total volunteer hours contributed and report back to Calderdale Council / community partners.
- [ ] Share photos and event summary on local community boards and social media.
- [ ] Send formal letter of thanks to the National Park Authority rangers and host team.
